Common questions from Canberra fathers

Straight answers — no jargon.

Do fathers have equal rights under Australian family law?

Yes. The Family Law Act 1975 is gender-neutral. Fathers have the same rights as mothers — to apply for parenting orders, to be involved in major decisions, and to seek time arrangements that are fair.

What matters in court is the best interests of the child — not which parent earns more or has historically done more of the day-to-day care. Your relationship with your children is the starting point.

My ex is stopping me from seeing my children. What can I do right now?

Document everything. Keep a record of every denied contact — dates, what was said, how. Send follow-up messages by text or email so there's a written trail.

If there are no current parenting orders, you can apply to the court for them. If there are orders and they're being breached, you can file a contravention application. In serious cases — where a child may be at risk or about to be moved — urgent orders can be heard within 24 to 48 hours. Call us.

How long does a parenting dispute take to resolve in Canberra?

It varies. Most matters resolve without going to trial — through negotiation, mediation, or consent orders — and those can be sorted in weeks to a few months. A fully contested hearing through the ACT Federal Circuit and Family Court typically takes 12–24 months from filing to a final order.

Early advice is the single biggest factor you can control. It almost always leads to faster, better outcomes.

Can I get equal time with my children?

Possibly — it depends on your specific circumstances. Courts do order equal time arrangements, particularly where both parents live close to each other and the child has a strong relationship with both. Equal time isn't automatic, but a meaningful and enforceable role in your children's life is a realistic goal.

How much does a family lawyer in Canberra cost?

It depends on how far the matter goes. Simple consent orders cost far less than contested hearings. At Mannion Lawyers, most stages are quoted at fixed fees so you know the cost before you commit. Book a consultation to understand your options and the realistic cost range before making any decision.

Do I have to go to court?

Most matters don't end up in front of a judge. Before filing, you're generally required to attempt Family Dispute Resolution (mediation). Many cases resolve there or through negotiation, ending with consent orders registered with the court — legally binding without a courtroom.

Where court is necessary, we'll be ready. But we always look for the best outcome, not the most expensive route to it.
